You may request an additional copy of this document at any time at no charge. The Home Mortgage Disclosure Act requires certain financial institutions,
including ABC Mortgage Company, to collect and report information about applications for, and originations and purchases of, home mortgage loans.
This notice is furnished to John Doe in connection with loan number 1234567890 and the property located at 456 XYZ Avenue, XYZ City, NY 12345. It
explains what information is collected, why the law requires it, how the information is disclosed to the public, and the limits that apply to its use. The data
reported under this statute play no part in the evaluation of your creditworthiness and cannot lawfully be used as a basis for any decision concerning
your application.

1.0 Purpose of the Statute
The Home Mortgage Disclosure Act was enacted to provide the public and public officials with sufficient information to determine whether financial institutions
are serving the housing needs of the communities in which they are located, to assist public officials in distributing public-sector investment so as to attract
private investment where it is most needed, and to assist in identifying possible discriminatory lending patterns and enforcing anti-discrimination statutes. The
statute is a disclosure and monitoring law; it does not direct any institution to make any particular loan.

The data collected under the statute are used by regulators, researchers, community organizations, and the public to examine lending patterns across
geographies, loan products, and applicant characteristics. Because the analysis depends upon complete and accurate reporting, ABC Mortgage Company
maintains procedures designed to ensure that the information recorded for each application, including the application associated with loan number
1234567890, is collected consistently and reported without alteration.

2.0 Complaints and Escalation
If you are dissatisfied with any aspect of the handling of loan number 1234567890, we ask that you first raise the matter with us by telephone at (555)
555-0120 or in writing at 100 ABC Plaza, XYZ City, NY 12345, describing the issue, the dates and amounts involved, and the resolution you seek. Most
concerns are resolved fastest at that level, and a written summary from you is the most reliable way to ensure the matter is understood as you intend it.

You may also, at any time and without first contacting us, submit a complaint to the federal consumer complaint system, to the state regulator having
jurisdiction over the institution in NY, or to the federal banking agency that supervises us. Raising a concern with us does not waive, shorten, or otherwise limit
any right you have to pursue the matter with a regulator or through any other remedy available at law or in equity.

3.0 Communication Preferences
You may tell us how you prefer to be reached, and we will honor a stated preference where the nature of the communication and applicable law permit. Certain
communications must be delivered in a specific manner regardless of preference, and those are sent by the required method even where you have asked to be
contacted only in another way.

Marketing communications are distinct from servicing communications. You may opt out of marketing by contacting us at (555) 555-0120 or by following the
instructions contained in the communication itself, and doing so does not affect the delivery of statements, disclosures, escrow analyses, or any other
communication concerning the administration of loan number 1234567890.

Where you have asked to receive communications electronically, keep the electronic address on file current and add our sending address to the list of
permitted senders in your mail application, so that a notice is not diverted to a junk folder. A notice diverted in that manner remains effective as of the date it
was sent.

4.0 Effect of a Transfer of Servicing
The servicing of loan number 1234567890 — the collection of payments, the administration of any escrow account, and the handling of your inquiries — may
be transferred to another company at any time. A transfer of servicing is not a sale of your obligation to a different borrower and does not change the interest
rate, the payment amount, the maturity date, or any other term of the note or the security instrument.

Where servicing transfers, you receive a notice from the transferor at least fifteen (15) days before the effective date and a notice from the transferee within
fifteen (15) days after it, unless the transfer occurs in circumstances for which the law permits a combined or later notice. The notices identify both companies,
the date payments should begin going to the new servicer, and the telephone numbers at which each may be reached.

For sixty (60) days after the effective date of a transfer, a payment received by the prior servicer on or before its due date may not be treated as late merely
because it was sent to the wrong company. Even so, redirect your payments promptly, and update any automatic payment arrangement you maintain through
your own financial institution, since that arrangement is under your control rather than ours.
