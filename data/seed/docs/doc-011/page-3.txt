         (p) Pre-Purchase Education
         Pre-purchase homebuyer education covers the mechanics of a mortgage transaction, the difference between prequalification and underwriting, the role of the
         appraisal and the inspection, the composition of closing costs, and the ongoing cost of ownership including taxes, insurance, maintenance, and utilities. It is
         offered as a group workshop, as an individual session, and, increasingly, as an online course.
         Several down-payment assistance programs and certain loan products condition eligibility on completion of an approved course and issuance of a certificate.
         Where such a condition applies to your transaction, complete the course early, since certificates are sometimes issued days after the session and a delay at
         the end of a transaction is avoidable.

         (r) Post-Purchase and Financial Counseling
        Post-purchase counseling addresses budgeting for a household that now carries a mortgage, planning for maintenance and replacement of major systems,
        evaluating whether a refinance is worthwhile, understanding property-tax assessments and exemptions, and reviewing insurance coverage periodically.
        Sessions are typically short and are frequently free.
        Approved agencies also provide general financial counseling covering credit, debt management, and savings. A counselor can help you evaluate whether a
        proposed consolidation, a home equity product, or a refinance offer that arrives in the mail is actually to your advantage — an assessment that is difficult to
        make from marketing material alone.
        (s) What Counseling Does Not Do
        A counseling agency does not lend money, does not approve or deny credit, does not negotiate the terms of your loan with us on its own authority, and cannot
        compel any lender or servicer to grant a request. Its role is advisory, and its value lies in the independence and experience of the counselor rather than in any
        authority over the transaction.

        agreement executed by the parties, and you should treat a counselor's general description of what is typically available as guidance rather than as a
