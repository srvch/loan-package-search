         Section 2. Computation of Time Periods
        Unless a particular statute, regulation, or provision states otherwise, a period expressed in days is measured in calendar days, the day of the act or event that
        starts the period is excluded, and the last day is included. Where the last day falls on a Saturday, a Sunday, or a legal public holiday, the period runs to the end
        of the next day that is not one of those days.
        Several requirements applicable to residential mortgage transactions are measured in business days, and the definition of a business day differs among them:
        one rule counts days on which our offices are open for substantially all business, while another counts all calendar days except Sundays and legal public
        holidays. Where a specific count controls a right or a deadline, it is stated in the provision that establishes it.
        Where a period is measured from the delivery of a document, delivery by mail is generally presumed to occur three business days after mailing unless earlier
        receipt is documented. Because these conventions determine real deadlines in your transaction, confirm the applicable dates with us or with your settlement
        agent rather than estimating them.

         Notify us promptly of any change in your mailing address, telephone number, or electronic address, and of any change in the name in which the account is

          Section 7. What a Request for Information Must Contain
          A request for information must state the name of the borrower, information that enables the servicer to identify the account, and the information sought. A
          request may seek documents such as a payment history, an escrow analysis, a copy of the note, or the identity of the owner or assignee of the loan. A request
          seeking the identity of the owner or assignee is subject to a shorter response deadline than other requests.
          A servicer is not required to respond to a request that is overbroad or unduly burdensome, to one that seeks confidential, proprietary, or privileged information,
          to one that is irrelevant to the servicing of the loan, or to one that is duplicative of a request already answered. Where a response is declined on one of those
          grounds, the servicer notifies you in writing and states the basis.

          Section 9. Acknowledgment and Response Deadlines
          The servicer must acknowledge receipt of a notice of error or a request for information in writing within five (5) business days, excluding legal public holidays,
          Saturdays, and Sundays. For a notice of error, the substantive response is due within thirty (30) business days, subject to a fifteen-business-day extension in
          most cases where the servicer notifies you of the extension and the reason for it.
          Shorter deadlines apply to particular categories. An assertion that the servicer failed to provide an accurate payoff balance must be answered within seven (7)
          business days, and a request for the identity of the owner or assignee of the loan must be answered within ten (10) business days. An asserted error
          concerning a scheduled foreclosure sale must be answered before the sale or within the general period, whichever is earlier.

          Section 11. How the Servicer Responds
          In responding to a notice of error, the servicer must either correct the error and notify you in writing of the correction, the effective date, and the contact
          information for further assistance, or conduct a reasonable investigation and notify you in writing that no error occurred, stating the reasons and how you may
          request the documents relied upon. Those documents are provided without charge upon request.
          A response to a request for information provides the information sought and contact information for further assistance, or explains that the information is not
          available, states why, and identifies the contact for follow-up. Responses are sent to the address of record unless you designate another address in your
          submission.
          Section 12. Complaints and Escalation
          If you are dissatisfied with any aspect of the handling of loan number 1234567890, we ask that you first raise the matter with us by telephone at (555)
          555-0120 or in writing at 100 ABC Plaza, XYZ City, NY 12345, describing the issue, the dates and amounts involved, and the resolution you seek. Most

          Section 13. Protections While a Submission Is Pending
          For sixty (60) days after receiving a notice of error concerning an asserted payment error, the servicer may not furnish adverse information to any consumer
          reporting agency regarding the payment that is the subject of the notice. This protection applies to the specific payment disputed and does not suspend the
          obligation to make other payments as they come due.

          Section 14. Practical Guidance for Submissions
          Submit a separate writing for each matter rather than combining several unrelated issues, state the loan number and property address at the top, describe the
          facts in chronological order, identify the specific dates and amounts involved, and enclose copies of the supporting documents. Keep a copy of everything you
          send, and send by a method that provides evidence of delivery.
         Do not include a notice of error on or with a payment coupon or check, because payment processing is automated and a notice enclosed with a remittance
         may not reach the correct department. Where the matter is urgent, a telephone call to (555) 555-0120 in addition to, rather than instead of, the written
          submission is often helpful.
          Section 15. Escalation and Other Remedies
         If you are not satisfied with the response, you may submit an additional notice identifying why the response is inadequate, and you may file a complaint with
          the federal consumer complaint system, with the NY regulator having jurisdiction over the servicer, or with the applicable federal banking agency. These
         procedures do not limit any right you have to pursue a remedy in court.
          Where a loan is transferred to a new servicer, a pending notice of error or request for information should be resubmitted to the transferee servicer at its
          designated address, because the transferor's obligation ends with respect to matters arising after the transfer date stated in the notice furnished to you.

          Section 18. Timing Around a Loan Transfer or Payoff
          A servicer’s obligation to respond ends with respect to a submission received more than one year after the loan was discharged or after servicing was
          transferred, and a submission received during that period is answered by the servicer to whom it is properly directed. Address a submission to the party that
          serviced the account during the period in question.
          Where a submission concerns a payoff figure and the loan has since been paid, the seven-business-day deadline applicable to payoff assertions continues to
          govern. Retain your payoff statement, the remittance confirmation, and the paid-in-full acknowledgment, since those three documents resolve nearly every
          question that arises after a loan is satisfied.
