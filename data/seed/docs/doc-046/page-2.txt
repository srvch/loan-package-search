Article VII. Identity Verification Requirements
Federal law requires all financial institutions to obtain, verify, and record information identifying each person who opens an account, in order to help the
government fight the funding of terrorism and money-laundering activities. When you apply for credit, we ask for your name, address, date of birth, and other
information that allows us to identify you, and we may ask to see a driver's license or other identifying document.

The information collected for this purpose is used to verify identity and is retained in accordance with applicable law. It is not used as a factor in evaluating
creditworthiness, and requesting identifying documentation is a routine step applied to every applicant rather than an indication of any concern about a
particular transaction.

Article VIII. Payment Methods and Crediting
Payments may be made by mail to the remittance address shown on your statement, by automatic monthly debit, by single online payment, or by telephone
where that service is offered. A payment received in the manner and at the location specified on your statement on or before the due date is credited as of the
date of receipt, even if the servicer's internal processing is completed on a later date.

A payment made by an unauthorized method, sent to an incorrect address, or tendered without sufficient identifying information may be delayed. Where a
payment cannot be identified, the servicer attempts to locate the account and, failing that, returns the funds to the sender. Always include the loan number on
any check or money order.

Article XI. Late Charges and Returned Items
If a check, draft, or electronic transfer is returned unpaid for any reason, the credit previously given is reversed, the payment is treated as never having been
made, and a returned-item fee is assessed where permitted by law and the loan documents. After a returned item, the servicer may require that subsequent
payments be made in certified funds for a period.

A late charge is assessed when a payment is not received in full by the end of the grace period stated in your note. The charge compensates for the
administrative cost of handling a delinquent payment; it is not interest, not a penalty, and not consent to late payment. A late charge is never assessed on a
payment received in full and on time, and a charge assessed in error is reversed upon review.

Article XII. Computation of Time Periods
Unless a particular statute, regulation, or provision states otherwise, a period expressed in days is measured in calendar days, the day of the act or event that
starts the period is excluded, and the last day is included. Where the last day falls on a Saturday, a Sunday, or a legal public holiday, the period runs to the end
of the next day that is not one of those days.

Several requirements applicable to residential mortgage transactions are measured in business days, and the definition of a business day differs among them:
one rule counts days on which our offices are open for substantially all business, while another counts all calendar days except Sundays and legal public
holidays. Where a specific count controls a right or a deadline, it is stated in the provision that establishes it.

Where a period is measured from the delivery of a document, delivery by mail is generally presumed to occur three business days after mailing unless earlier
receipt is documented. Because these conventions determine real deadlines in your transaction, confirm the applicable dates with us or with your settlement
agent rather than estimating them.

Article XIII. Suspense Balances and Payoff
When the loan is paid in full, any amount remaining in suspense is applied to the payoff or refunded to you, and the payoff statement identifies how a suspense
balance is being treated. A suspense balance is not automatically deducted from the payoff figure quoted to a third party, so the settlement agent should
confirm the treatment before disbursing funds.

If servicing is transferred, funds held in suspense transfer with the loan and continue to be held under the same terms by the transferee servicer. The transfer
notice furnished to you identifies the date after which payments should be sent to the new servicer.
