          Article |. Real Estate Taxes
          General real estate taxes are assessed by the county or municipality in which the property lies and are billed on a cycle established by local law. Unpaid taxes
          become a lien on the property that generally takes priority over the mortgage, and continued nonpayment can result in a tax sale of the property or of the tax

         Where an escrow account is maintained, the servicer pays the general tax bill from escrow. You should nonetheless review each assessment notice and tax bill
          you receive to confirm that the assessed value, the exemptions applied, and the parcel identification are correct. An error in any of those items affects the
         amount escrowed and is corrected through the assessor, not through the servicer.
         Article ll. Required Hazard Insurance
         The security instrument requires that the improvements be insured against loss by fire, hazards within the term "extended coverage,” and other hazards for
         which insurance is required, in the amounts and for the periods required, with a carrier acceptable to the lender. The policy must name the lender and its
         successors and assigns as mortgagee so that notice of cancellation is received and the lender's interest is protected.
         Coverage must be maintained continuously. A policy cancelled for nonpayment, not renewed at expiration, or reduced below the required amount leaves the
         collateral unprotected and permits the servicer to place coverage at your expense after the notices described in the separate lender-placed insurance notice.
         Confirm at each renewal that the mortgagee clause names the current servicer at the current address.

         Where the property is located in a special flood hazard area, flood insurance is required by federal law for the term of the loan, in an amount at least equal to
         the lesser of the outstanding principal balance, the insurable value of the improvements, or the maximum available under the National Flood Insurance
         Program. The determination applicable to your property is disclosed to you separately.
         Depending on the location and characteristics of the property, other coverage may be required or advisable, including windstorm or named-storm coverage in
         coastal areas, earthquake coverage where the exposure warrants it, and a walls-in policy where a condominium or homeowners association maintains a
         master policy. Review the association's coverage where applicable to confirm what it does and does not insure.
