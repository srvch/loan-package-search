15. Reading the Determination Form
The Standard Flood Hazard Determination Form identifies the lender, the borrower, and the property; states the community name and identifier; gives the map
panel number and its effective date; and records whether the improvements are in a special flood hazard area and whether the community participates in the
federal program. Review each field against the property you are financing.

The determination applies to the improvements rather than to the parcel as a whole. A property whose lot is partly within a mapped flood zone but whose
dwelling is outside it is not subject to the mandatory purchase requirement, which is one reason the location of the structure matters more than the boundary
drawn across the land.

16. Fraud Awareness and Reporting
Mortgage transactions attract fraud, most commonly in the form of messages that appear to come from a lender, a settlement agent, or a real estate
professional and that direct funds to an account controlled by a criminal. We do not transmit wiring instructions by electronic message, and we do not change
previously issued remittance instructions by electronic message. Verify any such instruction by telephone at a number you have independently confirmed
before transferring funds.

Report a suspicious communication concerning loan number 1234567890 to us at (555) 555-0120 promptly, and preserve the message rather than deleting it.
If funds have already been transmitted, contact your financial institution immediately and ask that a recall be initiated; the prospects of recovery diminish
sharply within hours, and prompt action is by far the most important factor.

17. Cost of Coverage and Elevation
Premiums under the federal program reflect the flood risk of the structure, including its elevation relative to the base flood elevation, its foundation type, and the
location of its lowest floor and machinery. An elevation certificate prepared by a licensed surveyor documents those characteristics and may materially affect
the premium.

Coverage is also available in zones outside the special flood hazard area, frequently at a lower premium, and a substantial share of flood claims arise from
properties outside mapped high-risk zones. Standard homeowners policies exclude flood damage, so the absence of a federal requirement is not a reason to
assume the risk is covered elsewhere.

18. Private Flood Insurance
Coverage from a private carrier may be accepted in satisfaction of the mandatory purchase requirement where it meets the definition of private flood insurance
established by federal regulation, or where it satisfies the discretionary acceptance criteria that permit a lender to accept a policy providing sufficient protection
consistent with safety and soundness.

If you wish to place coverage privately, provide the policy or binder early so that acceptability can be confirmed before closing. Private coverage may offer
higher limits or broader terms than the federal program, and it may also carry different renewal and cancellation practices; compare the policies rather than the
premiums alone.
