This notice explains what a credit score is, how it is used, and the rights you have with respect to the underlying consumer reports as of November 14,
2024.

Section 1. Understanding Your Score
A credit score is a computer-generated number that summarizes your credit record at a point in time and estimates the relative likelihood that an obligation will
be repaid as agreed. Scores are calculated by, or on behalf of, consumer reporting agencies using statistical models applied to the information in the file that
the agency maintains about you. They are not calculated by ABC Mortgage Company.

Because each agency maintains its own file, and because several scoring models are in commercial use, the score obtained from one agency may differ from
the score obtained from another on the same day, and a score obtained under a mortgage model may differ from a score you have seen through a consumer-
facing service. A difference between such figures does not by itself indicate that any file is inaccurate.

Section 2. The Role of Scores in Underwriting
Credit scores are one of several factors considered in a mortgage credit decision. Underwriting also weighs income and its stability, the ratio of housing and
total obligations to income, the funds available for the transaction and for reserves, the value and condition of the collateral, and the requirements of the loan
program and of any investor or insurer. A score is a summary indicator, not the decision itself.

Scores may also affect the pricing of credit. Where a loan program applies price adjustments based on the combination of credit score and loan-to-value ratio,
those adjustments are reflected in the interest rate or in the charges disclosed on your Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure. The composition of your rate is
disclosed on those documents rather than in this notice.

Section 3. Joint Borrowers and Multiple Obligors
Where more than one person is obligated on loan number 1234567890, each of you is jointly and severally liable for the entire obligation. That means the full
amount may be enforced against any one of you, against some of you, or against all of you together, without any requirement that other obligors, guarantors, or
collateral be pursued first, and a release or accommodation extended to one of you does not impair the liability of the others.

Any communication we deliver to one of you is treated as delivered to all of you, and an instruction, election, or authorization given by one of you may be acted
upon as though given by all of you unless the loan documents or applicable law require otherwise. Where your instructions conflict, we may decline to act until
the conflict is resolved among you in writing.

A change in the relationship among obligors — including a separation, a divorce, a transfer of an interest in the property, or the death of a borrower — does not
by itself alter the obligation or release any person from it. Notify us of such an event so that our records reflect the correct parties, and consult your own
attorney concerning the effect of the event on the ownership of 456 XYZ Avenue, XYZ City, NY 12345.

Section 4. Key Factors Affecting Your Score
The score-disclosure page accompanying this notice lists the key factors that adversely affected each score, in order of importance, as reported by the agency
that produced it. Common factors include the presence of derogatory information, the length of time accounts have been established, the proportion of
revolving balances to available credit limits, the number of recent inquiries, and the mix of account types in the file.

These factors are generated by the scoring model, not by ABC Mortgage Company, and we cannot alter them or recompute a score. A factor is a description of
what most influenced the number on the date it was calculated; it does not necessarily identify anything improper in the file, and a factor may appear even
where the score is high.

Section 5. Effect of a Transfer of Servicing
The servicing of loan number 1234567890 — the collection of payments, the administration of any escrow account, and the handling of your inquiries — may
be transferred to another company at any time. A transfer of servicing is not a sale of your obligation to a different borrower and does not change the interest
rate, the payment amount, the maturity date, or any other term of the note or the security instrument.

Where servicing transfers, you receive a notice from the transferor at least fifteen (15) days before the effective date and a notice from the transferee within
fifteen (15) days after it, unless the transfer occurs in circumstances for which the law permits a combined or later notice. The notices identify both companies,
the date payments should begin going to the new servicer, and the telephone numbers at which each may be reached.

For sixty (60) days after the effective date of a transfer, a payment received by the prior servicer on or before its due date may not be treated as late merely
because it was sent to the wrong company. Even so, redirect your payments promptly, and update any automatic payment arrangement you maintain through
your own financial institution, since that arrangement is under your control rather than ours.
