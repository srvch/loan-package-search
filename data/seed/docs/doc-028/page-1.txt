Please read this document in its entirety and retain it with the other papers relating to your loan. This disclosure explains how an escrow account is
established, funded, analyzed, and adjusted in connection with loan number 1234567890 and the property located at 456 XYZ Avenue, XYZ City, NY
12345. An escrow account is a fund held by the servicer from which property taxes, hazard insurance premiums, and similar recurring charges are paid
on your behalf as they come due. The estimates used to establish your account appear on the initial escrow account statement furnished separately;
this document describes the rules that govern the account for the life of the loan, including the cushion permitted by law, the annual analysis, and the
treatment of surpluses and shortages.

1.0 Purpose of the Escrow Account
An escrow account allows the annual cost of property taxes and insurance to be collected in monthly installments together with your principal and interest
payment, rather than in large periodic sums. The servicer holds those funds and disburses them to the taxing authority and to the insurance carrier on or before
the date each obligation becomes due, provided the account contains sufficient funds and the servicer has received the bill or the information necessary to pay
it.

Maintaining an escrow account protects both you and the holder of the security instrument. Unpaid property taxes can become a lien with priority over the
mortgage, and a lapse in hazard coverage leaves the property unprotected against loss. Because those risks are shared, escrow is required for many loan
programs and is offered as an option on most others.

2.0 Items Paid From Escrow
The items escrowed for your account are listed on the initial escrow account statement and typically include general real estate taxes, special assessments
levied by a taxing district, hazard or homeowners insurance premiums, and, where applicable, flood insurance premiums and mortgage insurance premiums.
Items that vary by jurisdiction, such as ground rent or a municipal service charge, may also be escrowed where the loan program requires it.

Charges that are not escrowed remain your direct responsibility. These commonly include homeowners association dues, utility charges, supplemental or
omitted tax assessments issued outside the ordinary billing cycle, and any insurance coverage you elect beyond what the security instrument requires. A
charge is not escrowed merely because a bill for it is received by the servicer, and the servicer will forward or return items it does not administer.

3.0 Initial Funding and the Cushion
At closing, an initial deposit is collected so that the account will hold enough to pay each escrowed item when it first comes due. Federal law permits the
servicer to maintain a cushion of no more than one-sixth of the estimated total annual disbursements, which is equivalent to approximately two monthly escrow
payments. Some loan programs and some state laws permit a smaller cushion, and where they do, the smaller figure controls.

The amount of the initial deposit depends on the month of closing relative to the tax and insurance due dates in NY, and it is calculated so that the projected
low point in the account balance over the coming twelve months equals the permitted cushion. The specific figures for this transaction appear on your Closing
Disclosure and initial escrow account statement and are not restated in this document.

4.0 The Yearly Analysis
At least once each year the servicer conducts an escrow account analysis, comparing the payments actually made from the account with those that were
projected and estimating the disbursements expected during the coming computation year. The analysis produces a new monthly escrow payment and an
annual escrow account statement showing the account history, the projected activity, and the resulting change to your total monthly payment, if any.

The annual statement is furnished to you at least thirty (30) days before the new payment amount takes effect. Escrow payments change most often because a
taxing authority has revised the assessed value or the rate applicable to the property, or because the insurance carrier has adjusted the premium at renewal.
Those amounts are set by third parties, and the servicer neither controls them nor profits from an increase.

5.0 Surpluses and Shortages
A surplus exists when the analysis shows that the account holds more than the target balance plus the permitted cushion. Where the account is current and the
surplus is fifty dollars or more, it is refunded to you within thirty (30) days of the analysis; a smaller surplus may be refunded or credited against future
payments. A shortage exists when the balance is below the target but the account is not overdrawn; you may pay a shortage in a lump sum or allow it to be
collected over the following twelve months.

A deficiency exists when the account balance is negative. Depending on the size of the deficiency and the status of the account, the servicer may require
repayment in a single sum or spread the amount over two to twelve months as permitted by law. In each case, the annual escrow account statement identifies
the condition of the account, states the amount involved, and explains the option being applied for loan number 1234567890.

6.0 What You Must Do
You remain responsible for maintaining the insurance coverage required by the security instrument and for ensuring that the servicer is named as mortgagee or
loss payee on each policy covering 456 XYZ Avenue, XYZ City, NY 12345. Notify the servicer promptly of any change in carrier, policy number, coverage
amount, or mailing address, and forward any tax or insurance bill that appears to require attention, particularly a supplemental assessment issued outside the
normal cycle.
