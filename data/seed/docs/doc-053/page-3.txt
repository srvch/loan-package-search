13. Types of Valuation Products
The valuation obtained for a transaction depends on the loan program, the property type, and the risk characteristics of the file. A full interior and exterior
appraisal is the most common product for a purchase of a one-unit dwelling; an exterior-only report, a desktop appraisal, a hybrid report incorporating a third-
party property inspection, or an automated valuation may be permitted where the program and the transaction allow. Each product is delivered to you under the
same rules.

In certain circumstances an appraisal waiver may be offered by an automated underwriting system on the basis of the data available for the property and the
parameters of the transaction. Where a waiver is offered and accepted, no appraisal is developed and none is delivered, because nothing exists to deliver. You
may nonetheless obtain an appraisal at your own expense if you wish to have an independent opinion of the value of 456 XYZ Avenue, XYZ City, NY 12345.

15. Second Appraisals and Reviews
A second appraisal may be required by the loan program, by an investor, or by our own quality-control procedures, and in defined circumstances it is required
by law, including certain higher-priced transactions involving a property acquired by the seller within the preceding one hundred eighty days at a lower price.
Where a second appraisal is required by that rule, its cost may not be charged to you.

We may also perform an internal review of a valuation, which may take the form of a desk review, a field review, or an automated quality check. A review is an
evaluation of the appraiser's work rather than an independent opinion of value, and it is not itself a written valuation requiring delivery unless it develops its own
value conclusion, in which case it is delivered to you like any other valuation.

16. Understanding the Report
An appraisal report identifies the subject property, describes its physical characteristics and condition, states the highest and best use, and analyzes recent
sales of comparable properties with adjustments for differences in location, size, condition, age, and amenities. The reconciliation section explains how the
appraiser weighed the indications of value produced by the approaches applied.

Pay particular attention to the description of the subject property, since a factual error there — an incorrect room count, square footage, lot size, or condition
rating — carries through the entire analysis. Errors of that kind are the most common and the most readily corrected, and identifying one promptly is the most
useful thing a borrower can do with a delivered report.
