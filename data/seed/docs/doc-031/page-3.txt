Article XV. Payoff in Connection With a Refinance
In a refinance, the new lender or its settlement agent orders the payoff statement, and the existing loan is satisfied from the proceeds of the new loan. Because
a refinance secured by your principal residence may carry a right of rescission, funding may occur several days after signing, and the payoff statement must
remain valid through the actual disbursement date rather than the signing date.

Do not stop making payments on the existing loan while a refinance is pending. Until the payoff funds are received and applied, the existing obligation
continues in full force, and a payment skipped in anticipation of a closing that is delayed produces a delinquency that may be reported and that may itself
jeopardize the new transaction.

Article XVII. After the Loan Is Paid
Following payoff you receive a paid-in-full acknowledgment, a refund of any escrow balance and overpayment, and, in due course, evidence that the lien has
been released of record. Any automatic payment arrangement you maintain should be canceled by you with your own financial institution, since a recurring
debit you established is not within the servicer's control.

Notify your insurance carrier and the taxing authority that the loan has been satisfied so that future bills and refunds are directed to you. Where an escrow
account existed, the next tax or insurance bill after payoff is your direct responsibility, and the transition is the point at which such an obligation is most often
overlooked.
