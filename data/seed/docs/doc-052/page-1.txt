This document describes the policy of ABC Mortgage Company concerning interest rate lock-in agreements for residential mortgage transactions,
including the application identified as loan number 1234567890 for the property at 456 XYZ Avenue, XYZ City, NY 12345. A rate lock is a commitment,
for a stated period and subject to stated conditions, to make credit available to John Doe at a specified interest rate and point combination. The specific
rate, points, lock period, and expiration date applicable to your transaction, if a lock has been established, appear on the separate rate lock confirmation
issued to you; this policy explains the rules that govern locks generally. This document supersedes any earlier version of the same document furnished
to you.

1.0 Floating Versus Locking
Until a lock is established, the rate applicable to your transaction floats, meaning that the rate available to you moves with the market and is not determined
until a lock is taken or, at the latest, until the point at which the loan documents are prepared. A floating rate exposes you to the possibility of both improvement
and deterioration in pricing before closing.

Locking fixes the rate and points for the lock period, transferring the risk of market movement during that period to the lender, which manages the exposure
through its own hedging arrangements. Neither choice is inherently better; the decision depends on your tolerance for uncertainty, the time remaining before
closing, and the state of the market when the decision is made.

2.0 Lock Periods and Expiration
Locks are offered for defined periods, commonly thirty, forty-five, or sixty days, and longer periods are available for certain programs at a price that reflects the
additional exposure. The lock expires at the close of business on the expiration date shown on the confirmation. A lock must be valid on the date the loan is
funded, not merely on the date the documents are signed.

Select a lock period that accommodates the realistic timeline of your transaction, including underwriting, appraisal, title work, and any condition likely to require
time to satisfy. A lock period that is too short frequently results in an extension fee that exceeds what the longer period would have cost at the outset.

3.0 How a Lock Is Taken
A lock is established only when we confirm it in writing. An oral discussion of available pricing, a rate quoted in a marketing communication, a rate shown on an
initial Loan Estimate issued before a lock, and a rate discussed in general conversation are not locks and create no commitment. If you have not received a
written lock confirmation identifying the rate, the points, the lock period, and the expiration date, your rate is floating.

Establishing a lock generally requires a complete application, a property address, and a loan program selection, because pricing depends on program,
occupancy, property type, loan amount, credit score, and loan-to-value ratio. A change in any of those characteristics after a lock is taken may require the lock
to be repriced under the terms described below.

4.0 Fraud Awareness and Reporting
Mortgage transactions attract fraud, most commonly in the form of messages that appear to come from a lender, a settlement agent, or a real estate
professional and that direct funds to an account controlled by a criminal. We do not transmit wiring instructions by electronic message, and we do not change
previously issued remittance instructions by electronic message. Verify any such instruction by telephone at a number you have independently confirmed
before transferring funds.

Report a suspicious communication concerning loan number 1234567890 to us at (555) 555-0120 promptly, and preserve the message rather than deleting it.
If funds have already been transmitted, contact your financial institution immediately and ask that a recall be initiated; the prospects of recovery diminish
sharply within hours, and prompt action is by far the most important factor.

5.0 Lock Extensions
Where the transaction cannot close before expiration, an extension may be available for a fee that depends on the length of the extension and the market at
the time it is requested. An extension request should be made before the lock expires; a request made after expiration is treated as a relock rather than an
extension and is priced accordingly.

An extension preserves the locked rate and points and changes only the expiration date. Fees for extension, where charged, are disclosed to you before the
extension is applied and appear on the revised disclosures issued for the transaction where required by the integrated disclosure rules.

6.0 Expiration and Relock
If a lock expires and the loan has not closed, pricing reverts to the market. A relock is generally offered at the worse of the original locked pricing and the
current market pricing, a convention that prevents a lock from operating as an option to be abandoned when the market improves. The terms of any relock are
confirmed to you in writing before they are applied.

Where expiration results from a delay attributable to us rather than to you or to a third party, the circumstances are reviewed and an accommodation may be
extended. Requests for such review should be directed to the office identified at the end of this policy and should describe the sequence of events giving rise to
the delay.
